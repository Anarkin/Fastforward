# ARCHITECTURE

- Only what nothing else can express is recorded here: a rule a test, a lint rule or a tsconfig can check belongs there instead, its reason in the name of its test

## Folders

- What each folder holds and what may import what are the tests of `app/src/test/architecture.test.ts` and `app/src/test/components.test.ts`, with the lint rules and the tsconfigs keeping each side from importing the other
- A file holds one concept: split it along its concepts once it takes a second

## Decisions

- The i18n dictionary, `app/src/lib/shared/strings.ts`, holds whole sentences, never parts to join, to support localization and globalization
- A dialog names the names, paths, branches, hashes, URLs and keys it is about on lines of their own after a sentence without them, the rest naming them in their sentence
- The page is not trusted: the main process checks everything the page sends before it acts on it, as the page shows content from outside the app
- Each window is a `BrowserWindow` loading a page of its own, rather than windows opened from one page, so a window can close without the others and no page code needs to know which document it draws in
- A tab dragged out of its row is run by the main process, as Chrome runs it, rather than by HTML drag and drop, so a window follows the pointer and the tab shows in the row it is dragged over; the window the tab left is kept see-through rather than hidden until the drop, as hiding or closing the window the pointer was pressed in ended the drag, and Electron has no move loop of the system to hand the window to
- In the `zustand` stores of the page: a store holds every action that posts, streams included, and any batching of them; the component that makes a store reads as little of it as it can, leaving the rest to its children; selectors return the narrowest slice, a primitive or a stable reference, never a whole sub-state of which only part is used, collections built in the reducer; a component takes as props what differs between its uses or comes from its parent's own state, and reads the rest from the store, passing elements rather than props down, none through more than one component that doesn't use it; new memos only for an obvious cost, as a pass over a list or a parse, or an identity something depends on
- The cursor of the text box with the focus is drawn by the page beside it, anchored to it by CSS anchor positioning, so it follows the box wherever it moves, the box's own caret hidden, as Chromium draws a block caret under the character in the character's own color and has no width for its bar
- Every scrollbar is one of the browser, drawn by the one skin of `app/src/shell/page/window.css`, rather than drawn by the page, so it takes its strip from the layout and looks alike in every tool; the page lays a cover nothing sees over the scrollbar under the pointer and takes its presses itself, as the browser steps and jumps by pixels, knowing nothing of rows, gliding or snapping, and no page can keep it from a press on its own scrollbar; a view that scrolls by itself, as a file viewed, the terminal and a text scrolled sideways, stands a scroller of the browser in for its scrollbar, as many times longer than itself as the view is than what it shows, and no longer than where its thumb is at its shortest
- A character that shows nothing or blank, a control, a format or direction character, a space but the space, is drawn as ¤ of the monospace font of the settings, and a sign of whitespace, the tab, a line end and the break of a wrapped row, as the character of that font, rather than by pictures or a font of the app, as two letters of a name drawn in a cell told no one what the character was and a font of the app needed making on every page; the dot of a space alone is drawn by the cells; where page text rather than cells holds a control character, as the prose of a Markdown preview, it is written into the page as ¤ with the character kept for a copy, as Chromium looks through every font of the system for a control character, finds none and lays out a line of them 60 times slower
- A tooltip is drawn by the page, one card for the whole app that a notice is drawn as too, rather than the `title` of the browser, which takes no path, no key and no setting and is drawn by the system at a size of its own; an element tells its tooltip in attributes and listens for nothing, one watcher of the page finding the element under the pointer, so a row of a list costs nothing for its tooltip and what the page did not draw itself, as a Markdown file, has the same tooltip
- The app icon is drawn once, from the few numbers of `app/src/shell/page/appIconDrawing.ts`: the page draws it as an SVG for the device pixels it takes, as the browser puts an SVG on whole device pixels itself, and `npm run icons` renders every icon file of `app/build/` from it with a rasterizer of its own rather than a library, so each size puts the edges between the bands of the drawing on whole pixels and the disc is cut from the whole picture, no band drawn under another showing through its rim, and a test renders them again and compares their pixels, which are the same on every platform
- Use `oxlint` instead of `eslint`, because `typescript-eslint` does not support TypeScript 7 yet
- Use `shiki` with its Oniguruma engine, the TextMate grammars and engine VS Code uses, so scope rules carry over from VS Code; semantic tokens are left out, as they need a language server
- The text of a file is to be drawn as cells on a canvas of the GPU, each glyph drawn once into an atlas and a screen of them in three draws, much as the terminal draws its screen, rather than as elements the browser lays out: first in the viewer, whose text and bytes have no elements any longer, then in the diff, whose code has none either, and then in the editor, whose text is seen as cells alone, the elements dropped as each followed; style, layout and paint were two thirds to four fifths of the main thread's work over the heavy files, and as cells the viewer took 2.5 ms at its worst frames over them rather than 7.8, the mean of its scenarios but opening, 9 of all 69 past the 5.5 ms of a frame at 180 Hz rather than 30, each of the 9 in the two files of wide characters never seen before, as a glyph takes a tenth of a millisecond the first time it is drawn
- A glyph of the cells is drawn by a canvas of the browser on the ground it stands on, in its color, and added to the screen as what it changed of that ground, so the subpixel edges of two glyphs that meet add up as the browser blends them, rather than cut out of its ground and laid over the screen, which dropped the edge of one where two met; by a canvas the GPU draws, as one the CPU draws was up to 8 levels off the text of the page; shifted by the part of a device pixel its text starts at, half a pixel at 125%, as Chromium draws text there; and with subpixel edges at every scale, as Chromium draws the text of the rest of the app, where the elements of the viewer had none from 150%, as Chromium gives them up there to scroll the kind of scroller they stood in on the compositor: a screen of cells is then the screen of elements to the pixel, but for the dots of spaces, a level off as the gradients that drew them dither, and a few dozen pixels where the edges of two glyphs meet; a tint or a mark that is see-through is blended as the GPU of Chromium blends a fill, the color rounded to bytes with its share worked in before it is laid over what is under it, a level off blending the unrounded color
- The cells are drawn with WebGPU, the newer way a page draws with the GPU, rather than with WebGL, which the terminal draws with, taken from the start so the engine is never moved to it later, though a frame of three draws is no faster by it; the page has it only where Chromium trusts the GPU, and none drawn in software as WebGL is, so the tests of the cells start the app with the switches of `gpuWithoutOne`, which give the machines of CI, that have no GPU, WebGPU drawn in software, and the app shipped keeps to what Chromium allows: where Chromium allows none, the app says so at its start, in one dialog rather than in every view, and quits, as nothing of it is drawn without one, and a GPU lost for good after one drew is told in a notice saying to restart, the app quitting nothing on its own, as files edited may be unsaved and copies running
- The canvases of a window share one atlas of glyphs and one set of pipelines, each view shown having a canvas of its own, as a Markdown document shows as many code blocks at once as it has in sight; a code block holds a canvas only while it is in sight, and only for the rows and columns around what is, as a canvas can be no taller or wider than a texture of the GPU and a block may have thousands of lines; a theme switched keeps the glyphs drawn for the other, each ink on each ground a style of its own, so a view restyled never wipes the glyphs another view draws with
- The code of a diff is drawn on a canvas for each side, which holds the rows in sight and half a screen or more above and below them and is scrolled by the browser with the list, drawn anew only once the list comes near its edge, rather than on one canvas as large as the view drawn anew at each step, as the viewer has, since the diff scrolls a list of rows of many kinds by the browser: the headers of files, the notes, the images, the dividers of hunks and the hatching of a side without a line stay elements of that list, and a canvas that moved a frame after them would tear from them; a hidden row laid out as a line was, the ruler, tells where the numbers and the code of each side start, so the cells stand where the stylesheet puts a line; and the cells take every color from hidden elements of the classes the stylesheet colors, a line added, a changed word, a find, so the theme stays in the stylesheets and the settings
- A test learns what the cells show by asking their element, which tells the rows it shows with their text, their pieces of code and where each stands, where the frame draws a glyph and which spaces it dots, worked out only once asked, rather than from elements kept beside the canvas with the text of the rows, which every scroll would pay for, whose characters would stand where the browser lays them out rather than where the canvas draws them, and which drew marks of their own over the cells once; a screen reader is to get such a copy of its own once it is asked for, as `xterm.js` has one
- Markdown is rendered with `markdown-it`, which VS Code's preview uses too
- Mermaid diagrams in Markdown are drawn with `mermaid` on the page's main thread, as VS Code's preview draws them, since it measures text in the DOM and can't run in a worker
- PDFs are drawn with the viewer components of `pdfjs-dist` inside the page, not in an iframe; the page reads a PDF through PDF.js's range transport, as PDF.js asks for ranges itself only over HTTP

## Tools

### Git, `git`

- Use the installed `git` CLI for everything, rather than a library or a git bundled with the app
- The commit list is kept whole by the main process per worktree, rather than read a page at a time as it scrolls, so the list knows its full size and locations can jump to any commit's position
- The bubbles of a commit with more than one are put on their lines by the page, from widths measured once in a hidden row of copies of the bubbles and the lanes of its row the main process counts as it walks the graph, rather than wrapped by the browser, so the list knows how tall a commit is before it loads or draws it and the height it scrolls through never changes as it draws them; the browser wrapped them only once drawn, and a commit with many bubbles grew the list as it came into view; as the room of a line changes, the commits drawn take their new lines at once and the others once it has rested 150 ms or the list scrolls, as laying out every commit at each step of the column dragged rendered the list at each step, 6.2 ms of main work a step against 4.5 ms in a list of 20,000 commits crowded with bubbles
- The commit list is drawn on one canvas of the GPU by the engine of the cells, its rows, their subjects, authors and dates, their bubbles and the graph alike, rather than as elements the browser lays out, so the list holds no element for a row: only its spacer, which the browser scrolls, the hidden copies of a row and of each shape of a bubble the canvas reads its colors and places from, as the diff reads its colors from hidden elements, and one element under the pointer that tells a tooltip, the canvas telling what lies under the pointer, a row, a bubble, a button of a bubble or the dot of a merge; the canvas is as large as the view and held in it, drawn anew at each step of a scroll from what each row drew the first time it was in sight, a band of rows three screens tall that the browser scrolled with the list having cost the compositor a millisecond and a half more each frame to present a canvas that tall, and a touchpad half as long again to reach the screen, so a step of the wheel over a list of the history of the app took 15.2 ms of main work rather than 22 as elements, and the frames of a touchpad 1.5 ms rather than 2.6, its input reaching the screen in 11 ms rather than 16.6; the text of a row and a bubble are drawn by a canvas of the browser once into an atlas at the part of a device pixel they start at, as the cells draw a glyph, the icons of a bubble by a canvas the CPU draws, as the page fills a path a quarter of a row at a time where a canvas of the GPU covers it exactly; the graph draws a lane straight down and its dashes as rects on whole device pixels, its dashes in step down the whole list, and the turns between lanes, the dots and the rings of merges worked out from their distance in the shader, rather than as an SVG for each row, whose lines met those of the next row within a device pixel, which took an overlap of a pixel; a row is no option of a list box for a screen reader any longer, which is to get a copy of its own, as the text of the viewer is
- The files of a commit, a comparison or the working tree are drawn on one canvas of the GPU by the engine of the cells, as the commits are, the list holding no element for a row: only its spacer, which the browser scrolls, a hidden row the height of a row is measured from, the hidden rows the canvas reads its fonts, places and colors from, as the commits read theirs, and one element over the letter of a change under the pointer that tells its tooltip; the folders stuck at the top are drawn over the rows scrolled under them, which are cut off below them, as a tile of the GPU covers nothing it is drawn over; a row is drawn again only once its look, its place or the looks of the parts of its path changed, and a frame like the last one is not drawn at all, as the main process answers the looks of the paths of new rows while the list scrolls and each answer drew every row again; over 20,000 files a notch of the wheel took 31.5 ms of main work rather than 34.5 as elements, with 2 long tasks rather than 48 and frames of 3.8 ms at p95 rather than 7.3, the rest of a notch being the glide and the column keeping where it is scrolled to, which the elements cost as well, as a row of files is no more than a letter, a twisty and a name
- A viewer tab showing a file as git keeps a version of it saves the commit or the index and the file whose history it is beside its path, as the main process never reads the view of a tab; the viewer reads a copy git takes out of the blob, made anew whenever the tab shows, so a restart or a cleared cache loses nothing

### Commander, `commander`

- Folders are listed on Windows with `NtQueryDirectoryFile` through `koffi`, off the main thread, as Node can't read the hidden attribute or the size of files the system holds open, such as `pagefile.sys`
- Native calls go through `koffi`, COM interfaces too, called through their tables of functions, rather than an addon of our own, which would need a compiler and binaries built for each platform; the menu of Windows runs its own message loop on the main thread, as Explorer's does, so the app waits while it is open
- Folders that need an administrator are read by a helper, the app itself started elevated in a mode of its own, as Total Commander does, rather than by restarting the app elevated, which would elevate the shells and git too and cut off drag and drop with other windows
- Archives are read with the command line of 7-Zip, shipped with the app at a pinned version, rather than a library such as `@zip.js/zip.js` or `7z.dll` through `koffi`, as one program reads every format a file manager meets, alike on every platform, edits those it writes, and runs in a process of its own
- Flat view sorts and filters its files in a worker of the page, which hands back their order as indexes, as sorting millions of files on the page's main thread would stall every tool for seconds
- Duplicate files are hashed in worker threads of the main process, as hashing at the speed of an SSD, about 3 GB/s for SHA-256 on one core, would keep its main thread busy most of the time
- Folder sizes are counted in worker threads of the main process, each walking folders with blocking calls, as the main thread was busy 80 to 98% of a count through the lister, a count of `C:\Program Files` took 2.9 s rather than 0.8 s and one of `C:\Windows` 15.6 s rather than 8.8 s
- The details and the thumbnails of a side are drawn on one canvas of the GPU by the list canvas of the cells, as the files of git are, rather than as an element holding one text for each row and an element for each tile, so every list of the app is drawn by one engine: the list holds no element for a row or a tile, only its spacer, which the browser scrolls, the ruler a row's height is measured from, and the hidden rows of the classes a row had, which the canvas reads its colors, fonts, places, check box and icons from, so the stylesheet stays the look of a row; the row or tile under the pointer has one element of its own, which carries a drag out to other apps, as Electron starts a drag of files only from a drag of an element, the git button of a repository in the details and the tooltip of a size, while presses, clicks and menus are told by where they fall on the list, so a click needs no move of the pointer first, as a tap has none; a text is drawn where the browser drew it, shifted by the part of a device pixel the browser moves the canvas by to stand it on whole pixels, as the page lays a text out on the pixel the list starts at and the canvas starts on the next whole one, so a side is drawn to the pixel as its elements were but for a tick of a box, a mark of the filter a pixel off at some scales and the edges of an icon the canvas scales as the browser scaled it within a few levels; the icon of a file is fetched by the canvas once the list rests, as the element of its row asked for it, and kept decoded while the side lives, the 5,000 drawn last, and the thumbnail of a tile held and kept decoded while the tile is in sight, as its element held it; against the elements, over 20,000 files a step of the wheel took 28.7 ms of main work rather than 28.2 and a page held down 24.8 rather than 24.4, and over 1,000 thumbnails a step 38.8 rather than 38.1, with long tasks down by three fifths to three quarters and the frames of the wheel 4.8 ms at p95 rather than 6.1, so one engine costs the commander about half a millisecond a step; a row is no option of a list box for a screen reader any longer, which is to get a copy of its own, as the files of git are

### Editor, `editor`

- The editor keeps the state of CodeMirror 6, the text edited, its changes, its selections, its undo and its commands, and none of its view: the text is in no element of the page, every character seen is drawn as cells, as the viewer and the diff draw theirs, and every place on screen, of a cursor, of a press of the pointer, of a row up or down, of where a line wraps, is worked out on the grid of the cells, from an index of the rows of the text that counts the rows of each part of the text once and keeps the count of every part a change leaves as it was, so the height and the width the browser scrolls through are those of the whole text before a line of it is drawn; what is typed, and what an input method composes, comes through an EditContext of the browser on the empty element that takes the focus, and the keys, the clipboard and the pointer are taken by the editor itself; the view of CodeMirror kept the text around the screen in elements nothing saw, which the browser styled and laid out again as it scrolled, guessed the height of every line it had not drawn, which the editor patched in its inner state, in three places, to keep the scrollbar still, and wrapped and placed that hidden text its own way, which every line had to be held to the grid against, so over the heaviest file, against that view, a notch of the wheel took 23.9 ms of main work rather than 30.7 and 22 rather than 29.4 with its lines wrapped, the scrollbar dragged 8.3 rather than 14.5, a key typed 11.6 rather than 14.3 with 3% of its frames janky rather than 28%, and 50 MB of code wrapped are counted in a quarter of a second, a second where every line wraps
- The code edited is colored by `shiki` in the worker of the colors, with the grammars of the viewer and the diff, rather than by CodeMirror's Lezer grammars, so the three color alike
- The text edited lives in the main process, as CodeMirror's `Text` with its undo history kept as JSON, as saving the settings reloads every page and a tab moves to another window by its id, so the page can't own it

### Canvas, `canvas`

- A canvas is read as JSON Canvas 1.0, the open format of Obsidian's Canvas, by the main process, which leaves out a node or an edge it can't read rather than refusing the file, so a canvas another app wrote opens with all of it that can be drawn
- A cell of a canvas is as many pixels of the file as the cell of the monospace font of the settings at 100%, in whole pixels, rather than a cell of a size of its own, so a canvas is laid out in the cells it is drawn in, and looks otherwise with another font or size
- The lines of the nodes and edges of a canvas are box-drawing characters drawn by the canvas as rects of whole device pixels, their joints, light or double, worked out from the lines of each cell, as the terminals draw them, rather than glyphs of the font, whose │ and ║ are shorter than a row at a line height of 1.5 and left gaps between rows
- The text of a canvas, of a node, of the label of a group and of an edge, is laid out and drawn by the engine of the views, its rows wrapped as the views wrap them, its places worked out by the code the editor works them out with and its rows drawn by the row drawing of the views, rather than by a layout of its own, which gave an emoji one cell and a cursor one cell over a wide character; each text starts on a whole device pixel and steps its columns from there, as the text of a view does, and is drawn only in the stretches of a row whose cells it owns, the topmost node or label owning a cell, as the nodes of a canvas overlap and a view's text never does; a test compares a node with the viewer, pixel by pixel where both texts start at the same part of a device pixel and cell by cell where they don't
- The canvas of a tab lives in the main process as the JSON of its file, every field and node it can't read kept, the page applying none of its edits but drawing what it moves while it moves it and sending the edit once let go, and the text typed into a node change by change, drawing it as typed, the main process joining the changes of one typing into one undone at once, which the main process checks and applies, as saving the settings reloads the page and a tab moves to another window, and is written back as its file was, so a canvas another app wrote stays as it wrote it but for what was changed
- A canvas is laid out as a screen of the cells in view, its groups first, then its edges, then its other nodes over them, then the joints, arrows and labels of the edges, so a node covers an edge running under it, and an edge still meets the side it leaves

### Terminal, `terminal`

- Use `xterm.js` with its WebGL renderer on the page and `node-pty` for the shells, as VS Code does
- The canvases of `xterm.js` take their CSS size from their size in device pixels, as it rounds that to whole CSS pixels, so the browser stretched them by a fraction of a pixel, blurring what they show further from their left edge
